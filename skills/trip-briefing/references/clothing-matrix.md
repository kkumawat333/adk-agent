# Clothing matrix

Look up the row for the temperature band, then the column for the local hour
band. Use the cell as the **What to wear** line of the briefing — rephrase it,
do not paste it.

| | `early` | `daytime` | `evening` | `night` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `cold` | Insulated coat, hat, gloves — dawn is the coldest hour | Insulated coat and a warm layer under it | Insulated coat now; it will get worse | Heaviest coat you have, plus hat and gloves |
| `mild` | Jacket over a long sleeve; you will want it until mid-morning | Long sleeve, jacket you can carry | Jacket now, and a warm layer for later | Proper jacket — it feels colder than the reading |
| `warm` | Long sleeve for the first hour, short sleeve after | Short sleeve; sunglasses and sunscreen | Short sleeve, but carry a light layer | Light jacket — the warmth does not hold |
| `hot` | Light and breathable; the best hour to be outside | Light, loose, light-coloured; hat and sunscreen | Still hot — stay breathable | Light layers; it may stay warm all night |

## Adjustments

Apply on top of the cell above, in this order:

1. **Rain or showers** — add a waterproof outer layer and waterproof shoes.
   This outranks the cell's outer layer: a waterproof shell replaces a
   non-waterproof jacket rather than going over it.
2. **Snow or ice** — add boots with grip, and treat the temperature as one band
   colder than measured.
3. **Wind or "breezy"** — treat the temperature as one band colder, and prefer
   a windproof outer layer over an insulated but open one.
4. **Humid** — drop one insulating layer from the cell; humidity makes warm
   feel hotter and cold feel wetter.

When two adjustments apply, apply both, but keep the recommendation to a single
sentence in the briefing.

## Notes

- The bands come from the main playbook (`SKILL.md`); do not re-derive them.
- Every cell already accounts for its hour band, so do not shift a band for
  time of day on top of the lookup — that is what the columns are for.
- These are general-purpose recommendations, not a substitute for a local
  advisory during severe weather. If the weather report mentions a warning,
  storm, or extreme condition, lead the **Worth knowing** section with it.
